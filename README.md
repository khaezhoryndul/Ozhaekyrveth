# Ozhaekyrveth

> A black pearl holds the tide of a sky that never learned to rain.

## The Merchant of Empty Bottles

The merchant sets out her bottles before sunrise. They contain nothing visible, but each casts a different shadow: a narrow bridge, a sleeping dog, a hand holding an extinguished candle. She arranges them by the sounds they make when nobody is listening.

Her stall stands in a square paved with pale shells. No sea has reached the town for centuries. Still, the shells close slightly when a storm approaches, and people avoid stepping on them after dark.

The merchant owns one bottle without a shadow. It is sealed with a stopper of black wood and tied with a thread that cannot be cut. Inside lies a black pearl suspended halfway between the glass and its reflection.

She never offers this bottle for sale. She places it at the edge of the stall every morning and turns it toward the western road.

## A Buyer Carrying No Hands

A buyer arrived wearing gloves that moved without hands inside them. His sleeves were empty. The merchant greeted him as if they had met before, although she had not yet been born on the day he remembered.

He asked for the bottle that held the sound of a bridge. She gave him the one whose shadow resembled a sleeping dog. He nodded and placed a white stone on the counter.

The stone carried a wet mark in the shape of an eye. The merchant pressed it against the bottle containing the pearl. At once, the thread loosened and the stopper rose a little from the glass.

From within came the smell of snow on a warm roof. Above the square, the sky darkened into a shade of blue that the shells seemed to recognize. They opened beneath the buyer's feet without making a sound.

## The Tide Above the Town

The pearl contains a sky. Its clouds move slowly across a black sea, but the sea lies above the clouds and its waves break upward. Ships appear beneath its surface as pale scratches. None has a mast.

The merchant watches these ships through a lens made from frozen tears. She records their shapes on pieces of linen and sends the linen down the western road in bottles with no stoppers.

The bottles always return empty. Sometimes they return before she has sent them. On those mornings she keeps the stall closed and sits inside her house with every mirror facing the wall.

The buyer lifted the frozen lens. In the pearl, he saw a ship turn toward the edge of the sky. Beneath its hull moved a long shadow with wings wider than the clouds.

He lowered the lens. One of his empty gloves had become wet.

## The Passenger Beneath the Waves

The winged shadow belongs to a creature that never surfaces completely. A horn may rise between two waves. A line of scales may appear where the clouds are thinnest. Once, the merchant saw its closed eye and discovered the town reflected inside it.

In that reflection, the square was covered with water. Her stall had become a boat, and the buyer stood beside it with his hands resting on a pair of oars. The merchant herself was absent, but her shadow lay across the boat's empty seat.

She had searched the pearl every morning since then. The reflection never returned. The creature's eye remained closed, and the ships continued moving without wind.

Now the buyer placed his wet glove beside the white stone. Water spread across the counter. Within it appeared the town's western road, running straight into a sea that had never been drawn on any map.

## What the Bottle Heard

The black stopper bore marks along its underside. The merchant had seen them before but had never been able to read them. In the water on the counter, their reflection became clear:

*Ozhae — kyr — veth — unnal.*

The buyer touched each mark with an empty finger. The bottle that cast the shadow of a sleeping dog began to tremble. Its shadow stood, stretched, and walked toward the western road.

The merchant followed it with her eyes. For one moment, the road seemed made of glass, and beneath it she could see the pearl's black sea. The winged creature passed slowly below the houses. Its scales carried pale shells, each closed around a tiny light.

The stopper settled back into place. The thread tightened around the bottle's neck. The road became ordinary stone again.

## The Sale That Remained Unfinished

The buyer departed with the bottle he had requested, though its shadow had already gone ahead. He left both gloves on the counter. In one lay the white stone. In the other rested a small black feather, dry despite the water around it.

The merchant placed the feather beside the pearl. Through the glass, she saw the mastless ship turn once more toward the sky's edge. A figure now stood on its deck, holding a bottle against the light.

She opened her hand. On her palm was a wet mark in the shape of an eye.

The square remains paved with shells.

The bottles remain empty to anyone passing by.

But every morning, before the merchant opens her stall, the western road smells faintly of a tide that has begun to come home.
